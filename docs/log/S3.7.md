## Decisions

- **`category: embedded`, not `systems-hpc`.** The subject is a UART's
  sampling clock and the protocol layers stacked on it. Filing it under
  Systems would put it next to SpGEMM and mislabel both.

- **`status: in-progress`, and the page says why.** Levels 01–04 are drawn;
  05–11 are implemented and tested in the engine with no UI. Both the badge
  and "What I'd do next" state it plainly rather than letting the metrics
  imply a finished ladder.

- **No `/lab` route in this story — case study only.** S12.1 vendored the
  electrons instrument into the site and took a documented design-system
  exemption for its scoped palette. The same treatment is the right eventual
  answer here, but electrons was 11 rungs of 11 when it earned it and this is
  4 of 11. Shipping a quarter-built instrument into a page with a hard
  Lighthouse gate spends the F11 margin on something that would be replaced
  within weeks. Logged as F16 instead. The repo runs it in two commands, and
  the case study links there.

- **Lead the hard section with my own bug.** The strongest material is the
  receiver that timed samples from the previous bit and silently ran 6% fast
  — a real error, found during the build, whose fix is what made the
  tolerance derivation fall out. Per `content-voice` that beats any adjective
  about difficulty.

- **The unpredicted finding stays in.** The back-to-back-frames penalty was
  not designed for; it fell out of the bisection sweep and explains a piece of
  received embedded wisdom (8N2 on a marginal link). It is the one claim on
  the page that came from the instrument rather than into it.

## Follow-on: the lab route (same day)

Shao asked for the instrument route directly right after the case study
shipped, so F16 was resolved the day it was raised rather than waiting for
levels 05–11. `/projects/bits-to-messages/lab` measures 95/100/100/100.

**The exemption did not need widening.** `.electrons-scope` became
`.instrument-scope`, and both demos share it. They carry the identical six
meanings — amber is a settling logic level in one and an unacknowledged packet
in the other — and the CSS comment already called the block "Instrument scope",
so the class had been named for its first tenant rather than for its job.
Renaming it is four files; adding a second near-identical exemption would have
been a permanent second thing to keep in step. The `--e-` prefix stays as
historical, because renaming the variables would touch every electrons level
component on a shipped page for no reader benefit.

Verified rather than assumed, in the browser on the built export:

- The bits instrument paints `rgb(245,181,68)` — the site's CH1 amber — not its
  own `#f5b83d` fallback. The binding reaches pixels, so the exemption really is
  three colours and not six.
- The standalone Vite demo paints `rgb(245,184,61)`, its canonical fallback,
  with its own `<h1>` and `<main>` intact. Both halves of the contract hold.
- The electrons lab after the rename: `.instrument-scope` matched, background
  `#07090c`, all six `--e-*` resolved, zero stale `.electrons-scope` elements.

**Two real accessibility defects came out of the embed**, and both were wrong in
the source repo too. Controls were ~27px against the exemption's own
non-negotiable 44px. And the muted ink was `#6b7d75` — 4.40:1 on the panel,
quietly under AA — now `#8b9a93` at 6.5:1. The rate slider also inherited a
visible label carrying a live percentage, so its accessible *name* changed on
every drag; it has a stable `aria-label` now and lets the value do the talking.

The vendored copy is a subset: five core modules, closed under their own
imports. The page is 4.35 kB / 134 kB First Load against the electrons lab's
31.2 kB / 161 kB. Five new tests pin the parts worth pinning — the no-JS render
(deterministic initial state, so the trace and the decoded bytes belong in the
HTML), the landmarks under `chrome={false}`, keyboard operability, and level
04's actual claim: detune past +3.90% and the frames raise framing errors, come
back under it and they clear. 212 → 228 tests.
