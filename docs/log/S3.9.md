# S3.9 — bits-to-messages: levels 08–11, the ladder closes

Status: done · Started: 2026-08-21 · Shipped: 2026-08-21

Follow-on to S3.8, requested directly by Shao ("continue to level 08-11"). The
Smith & Nephew carve-out from S3.8 still stood, so S4.2 was not touched.

## What shipped

All eleven rungs are drawn. `/projects/bits-to-messages/lab` holds
**96/100/100/100** at 20 kB, unchanged from S3.8's 13.3 kB. The case study flips
from `in-progress` to `complete` and its metric row now leads with
`rungs: 11, each breakable`.

Levels 08 MESSAGE, 09 INTEGRITY, 10 LINK and 11 BUS. Three of them ran on engine
code that already existed; **level 11 needed new engine work**, since core had no
bus model at all.

## New in the engine

`packages/core/src/bus.ts` — RS-485 multidrop, plus Modbus RTU's frame timing.
13 tests. Two distinctions it makes that a simpler model would miss:

- A driver that is not enabled is **high impedance, not zero**, so the model has
  three states and an undriven bus reads mark from failsafe biasing rather than
  from absence of signal.
- Two enabled drivers are always a **conflict** and only sometimes
  **contention**. Agreeing drivers are electrically survivable; opposed ones are
  a low-impedance path from supply to ground held for the whole overlap. They are
  different faults and the model keeps them apart.

Modbus RTU is in there because it is the *other* answer to level 08's problem:
delimit by silence rather than by a reserved byte. 3.5 character times ends a
frame, 1.5 within one invalidates it, and above 19200 baud the specification
stops computing them and fixes them at 1.750 ms / 750 µs. Both regimes and the
boundary are asserted.

## The ladder's shape

10 LINK is the rung the whole thing was climbing towards, and it is the one to
read: lose an ACK, and a receiver that ignores sequence numbers delivers the
message twice while every layer below reports a healthy link. Both frames
intact, both CRCs valid, no flag raised anywhere. Integrity is not delivery.

11 BUS is a **coda rather than a rung stacked on top** — it breaks the two-ends
assumption every earlier level relied on rather than building on it, which is
the same shape the electrons project used for its MEMORY level.

The ladder stops at a shared bus deliberately, and both the instrument and the
case study say so: above multidrop the next honest layer is addressing and
routing, which is a different subject rather than a taller version of this one.

## Verified rather than assumed

Every failure was driven in the browser and read back before shipping:

| Level | Check | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 08 | chunk size 1 vs 40 | 3 delivered either way — chunking is the transport's business |
| 08 | listener joins mid-stream | exactly 1 frame discarded, next 2 delivered |
| 09 | flip one bit | XOR, sum and CRC all catch it |
| 09 | two flips, same column | XOR passes undetected; CRC catches |
| 09 | swap two bytes | XOR **and** sum pass undetected; CRC catches |
| 10 | ACK lost, seq checked | 1 retransmit, 1 duplicate recognised, delivered once |
| 10 | ACK lost, seq ignored | **delivered twice**, zero errors reported |
| 11 | driver held 16 bits | 6 conflict bits, 5 of them opposed — matches the master's frame exactly |

## Decisions

- **Level 11's slider got headroom.** The collision was only reachable at the
  final notch, which reads as a broken control rather than a threshold. Max
  raised to 20, and a `collides once held past` stat names the boundary instead
  of making the reader derive it from two other numbers.
- **`PENDING` stays as an explicit empty list** rather than being deleted. The
  instrument renders a note from it, and "there is nothing left" is a statement
  worth making rather than an absence.
- **The whole engine is vendored now** — twelve modules rather than seven. The
  earlier subsetting existed to keep unused layers off a budgeted page; with all
  eleven levels drawn, nothing is unused.
