# S3.11 — bits-to-messages: UART, SPI and I2C end to end

Status: done · Started: 2026-08-21 · Shipped: 2026-08-21

Requested directly by Shao: type a message in a terminal, watch it go out a
serial port, cross a wire, and be decoded at the other end — then do the same
for SPI and I2C. The Smith & Nephew carve-out from S3.8 still stood.

## What shipped

A **JOURNEY** mode: five stages carrying one message across, with the protocol
switchable. Characters → bytes → transitions on wires → a receiver deciding when
to look → characters again. `/projects/bits-to-messages/lab` holds
**96/100/100/100** at 26.5 kB.

SPI and I2C needed new engine work — core had neither.

## The unifying idea

A shared multi-signal trace type (`trace.ts`) so all three protocols are drawn
by one renderer. That is the argument rather than a convenience: on the same
axis they stop looking like three subjects and start looking like three answers
to one question — *how does the far end know when to look at the line?*

| | wires | knows when to look by | cannot detect |
|---|---|---|---|
| UART | 2 | an agreed rate + a start bit | which end's clock is wrong |
| SPI | 4 | a clock on its own wire | anything — no error detection at all |
| I2C | 2 | a clock, shared by every device | little; the 9th clock ACKs each byte |

## Engine notes worth keeping

- **SPI's failure is silent by design.** The slave samples on the edge its own
  mode nominates, so a CPHA mismatch produces genuinely wrong bytes — sampled
  half a clock from the data — with no parity, no ACK and no error flag
  anywhere. Modes 0 and 2 are asserted to *interoperate*, because only the
  clock's idle level differs and the sampling offset does not; that asymmetry is
  the kind of thing a hand-waved model gets wrong.
- **I2C's framing is free.** Both lines are open drain, and SDA may only change
  while SCL is low — so the two moments it moves during a *high* clock are
  available to mean something else, and they do: START and STOP. A third answer
  to level 08's boundary problem, alongside COBS and Modbus silence. A test
  walks the whole transaction asserting SDA never moves during a high clock
  except at those two points.
- **Nine clocks, not eight.** The ninth is the receiver's, and it is why I2C is
  the only one of the three that can report "nobody is at that address".

## Defects found by checking

- **The UART "break it" reported success.** I compared byte values only, and at
  6.4% out the stop bit lands wrong while the data bits still survive — so the
  link raised framing errors and the page said "arrived intact". Fixed to
  require zero flags, and the copy now leads with the better truth: drift
  reaches the stop bit first because error accumulates and it is sampled last,
  so *the first thing you lose is not your data, it is any reason to believe
  your data*. A 14% option shows the case where the data goes too.
- **MISO sat flat at zero**, reading as a dead wire and hiding that SPI has no
  half-duplex mode. The slave now holds it high the way a real one with nothing
  to say does, and the panel points at it.
- Copy said "the same five characters" regardless of the message. Now counted.
