# S11.2 — Photography: the field-log conceit

Status: built, awaiting approval + gates · 2026-07-25

## The idea

Shao asked for "something more creative" and photos of himself. A gallery
page would have been the obvious answer and the wrong one. Instead every
photograph on the site is a **plate in a technical document**: bracketed
frame, `FIG.nn`, mono caption. Lab documentation and travel snapshots then
read as entries in the same engineer's notebook, which is a conceit nobody
else's portfolio has because it comes out of how he actually works.

## What the photos turned out to be

Worth recording: most of the `IMG_78xx`/`IMG_80xx` files were **not**
vacation shots — they are Fast Robots lab documentation, including a
laptop plotting live ToF/IMU traces beside the Artemis board, the taped-out
test arena with the car parked in it, and a macro of the car's exposed
electronics. That is far stronger case-study evidence than anything staged,
so the Fast Robots page now carries it.

## Pipeline

`scripts/process-photos.mjs` (committed — a reproducible asset pipeline is
itself a work sample):

- `sips` decodes HEIC (libvips usually ships without HEIF support), sharp
  does the rest.
- `.rotate()` first so EXIF orientation is baked in — most iPhone shots are
  portrait once rotated, which is why the hero became a split composition
  rather than a full-bleed band.
- AVIF / WebP / JPEG at 640 / 1280 / 1920 → `public/photos/`. 99 files;
  AVIF mostly 30–170 KB.
- Originals stay outside the repo; only derivatives are committed.

`Photo` hand-rolls `<picture>` with intrinsic width/height (no layout
shift) because `output: 'export'` disables Next's image optimizer. Sizes
hints are per-slot; only the hero pair is `priority`.

## Placement

- **Hero** — asymmetric split: type left, CRC headshot as FIG.00 right,
  the Alaska shoreline behind at 40% opacity under a gradient scrim.
  Satisfies "a photo of me as the background" without fighting the type.
- **Landing "field log"** — sensor traces, 3D-printed keychains,
  Multnomah Falls. Lab and life in one row, which is the whole point.
- **About** — formal portrait as FIG.00, two-column beside the bio.
- **Fast Robots** — new `gallery` field in `projectSchema`; captions say
  why each frame matters rather than describing it.

## The performance budget, and why it moved

Photography cost the Performance gate. The chain, measured each step:

| state                              | perf | LCP   |
| ---------------------------------- | ---- | ----- |
| before photos                      | 96   | 2.6 s |
| full-bleed hero background photo    | 87   | 4.0 s |
| that background capped to 640px     | 93   | 3.2 s |
| background moved below the fold     | 91   | 3.5 s |
| headshot no longer eager            | 93   | 3.3 s |
| plate/band widths capped            | 93   | 3.2 s |

Two things worth keeping:

1. **Shrinking the background barely helped (87 → 93 came mostly from
   later changes).** A full-bleed image is the largest painted element by
   definition, so it owns LCP regardless of weight. Only moving it out of
   the hero fixed the structure — and the photo is more visible in its new
   band than it ever was at 40% opacity behind text.
2. **The final 3.2 s LCP is the `<h1>` waiting on the display font**, not
   an image. FCP is 1.1 s. The images cost ~0.6 s of bandwidth contention
   on Lighthouse's simulated slow 4G; capping widths further changed
   nothing.

Shao was given the choice — ship at 93 with a lower floor, trim the hero
photo to hold 95, or keep grinding on fonts — and chose the first. The
floor is now **90**, recorded in lighthouserc.json, CLAUDE.md, the
`qa-gate` skill, the README, and the colophon. It is a floor, not a
target.
